## 1. The role

### 1.1 Programme structure: is it an internship that converts to full-time?

- **Yes, an internship with a possible full-time offer, but conversion is not automatic.** The posting says: *"you will join a client service team for 8–10 weeks, contributing to workstreams."* The McKinsey China intern listings add: *"At the end of your internship, you may receive an offer to join as a full-time business analyst after completing your degree."*
- **Who it is for.** Eligibility is set by **graduation year ("class")**, not by year of study. In the current Greater China cycle (announced 17 Jul 2026):
  - **Summer Business Analyst (intern) = Class of 2028.** That means you graduate around Dec 2027–Aug 2028, so you are in your **penultimate year now** and would intern in **summer 2027**.
  - **Business Analyst (full-time) = Class of 2027.** That means you are in your **final year now** and would join after graduating in 2027.
  - **Exception:** a final-year undergraduate going straight into a **one-year master's** (graduating 2028) can count as Class of 2028 and apply as an intern ("in some geographies").
  - GCR deadlines this cycle:

    | Your university is in | Full-time BA (2027) | Intern (2028) |
    |---|---|---|
    | UK | **12 Oct 2026** | 14 Jan 2027 |
    | Mainland China | 23 Aug 2026 | 27 Dec 2026 |
    | Hong Kong | 23 Aug 2026 | 23 Aug 2026 |
    | North America | 11 Aug 2026 | 11 Aug 2026 |
    | Other | 23 Aug 2026 | 27 Dec 2026 |

  - The same pattern held in 2024: GCR recruited "BA for class of 2025" alongside "BA Intern for class of 2026".
  - **If you are final-year with no master's planned, raise it with Daphne** and ask about the full-time BA track. The UK deadline for that track is 12 Oct 2026.
- **Timing.** GCR runs summer internships plus **off-cycle "Start ASAP" internships** in Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen. Off-cycle hires are made year-round, so this one could be either.
  - Daphne's email says "Business Analyst position" while your link is the BA *Intern* posting. **Ask her which track and start window you are being considered for** (see §2.7).
- **Conversion rates.** US prep sites estimate that roughly 80–90% of US summer interns get return offers. That figure is unverified and not GCR-specific, so treat it as directional only.
- **Career path after converting.** BA → Associate in about 2–3 years. Promotions in GCR can be fast (Chinese prep sources say about 2 years from generalist BA to Associate).

### 1.2 Industry and focus: finance? growth? strategy?

- **This is a generalist consulting role, not a finance role.** You are staffed onto whatever live client engagement needs you. Staffing is managed by a **professional development manager**, who weighs your interests against team needs.
- GCR lets campus applicants apply as a **Generalist** or with **Practice preferences** (e.g. Operations, Digital/QuantumBlack). Shanghai also hires dedicated "Business Analyst – Operations" roles. Interns are usually generalists.
- **Typical topics** you could land on:
  - Growth and go-to-market strategy for a consumer or luxury brand
  - A "China for China" operating model for an MNC
  - Overseas expansion for a Chinese private company
  - Cost and operations transformation for a manufacturer under price-war pressure
  - EV/auto consumer strategy
  - Financial-institution strategy
  - Organisation design
  - Digital/analytics (traditional AI) work
- **Shanghai** is GCR's largest office. Third-party guides describe it as strong in **consumer and retail, financial services and multinationals**. It also hosts McKinsey's **Shanghai Client Capabilities Hub** with 250+ specialists across auto, consumer, chemicals, energy, financial institutions, high tech, pharma and travel/logistics, plus an advanced analytics group.
- **How to answer "which area interests you?"** Name one or two genuine interests (e.g. consumer growth or Chinese brands going global) and say you are **open to breadth**. Recruiters want to hear that you would be happy as a generalist.

### 1.3 Skills required (from the posting)

- Undergraduate degree in progress (any major: engineering, economics, sciences and humanities are all common)
- **Strong verbal and written communication in English and the local office language (Mandarin for Shanghai)**, adjusted to different seniority levels
- Decisions driven by data, facts and logical reasoning
- Producing work products: **PowerPoint decks, Excel models**, written deliverables
- Self-starting organisation and time management in a largely autonomous environment
- Willingness to work outside normal hours or take on projects at short notice
- Willingness to travel and work on site (factories, hospitals, mines)

### 1.4 What a day looks like

You are a full member of a team of usually 3–5 consultants (an Engagement Manager, one or two Associates/BAs, and you), and you **own a piece of a workstream**. A typical week:

- **Team problem-solving sessions:** the team forms hypotheses together and you get a specific question to answer, e.g. "size the premium pet-food market in tier-1 cities".
- **Research and data:** desk research, company and industry data, Excel analysis, often with internal knowledge tools such as **Lilli**, McKinsey's gen-AI platform used by more than 70% of staff.
- **Interviews:** expert calls, client interviews, sometimes customer surveys or site visits.
- **Synthesis:** turning findings into "so-what" pages in McKinsey's deck style, with daily check-ins with your EM.
- **Client meetings:** presenting your pages to client counterparts. Interns often present in steering committee prep or working sessions.
- **Feedback:** frequent coaching plus a formal review at the end, which feeds the return-offer decision.
- **Hours** are long and sometimes unpredictable, and you may be at the client site for part of the week.

### 2.1 Where this call sits in the process

### 2.3 What Daphne is evaluating

- **Motivation:** do you really understand and want consulting and McKinsey Shanghai specifically?
- **Communication:** concise, structured, top-down answers. Lead with the conclusion.
- **Credibility:** you know every line of your CV, with dates and numbers.
- **Fit and potential:** early signs of personal impact, drive and leadership.
- **Feasibility:** graduation date fits the programme, you are available when needed, you have the language, and you can work in mainland China.

### 2.4 Prepare these stories (STAR, with numbers)

McKinsey's experience questions test **Personal Impact**, **Entrepreneurial Drive** and **Inclusive Leadership**, plus the newer **Courageous Change** (adapting to ambiguity or change). Prepare one story for each. You will probably only use one or two tomorrow, but they are essential for round 1.

| Dimension | Prompt you might hear | Your story (fill in) |
|---|---|---|
| Personal impact | "Tell me about a time you persuaded someone who disagreed with you." | |
| Entrepreneurial drive | "Tell me about a time you pursued an ambitious goal and hit obstacles." | |
| Inclusive leadership | "Tell me about a time you led a diverse team through a difficult situation." | |
| Courageous change | "Tell me about a time you faced significant change or ambiguity." | |
| Failure / learning | "Tell me about a time something didn't go as planned." | |

Use this structure: **Situation (1 sentence) → Task / why it was hard (1–2 sentences) → Actions (3 specific ones, saying "I" not "we") → Result (number) → What you learned.** Expect follow-up questions such as "what exactly did you say?", "why that approach?" and "how did the other person react?"

### 2.5 Logistics: have exact answers ready

- Degree, university, year of study and **expected graduation month and year**
- Whether you are doing a master's afterwards (this affects eligibility)
- **When you could intern and for how long** (summer 2027? earlier off-cycle?) and how flexible you are
- Mandarin and English level; any third language
- Willingness to be based in Shanghai and to travel; work eligibility in mainland China
- Other firms or processes in progress, and any deadlines
- Practice preference, if any (and that you are open to being a generalist)

### 2.6 If you get a quick estimate (60-second approach)

1. **Clarify** the question (scope, unit, time period).
2. **State the structure** before doing any maths: *"I'll estimate from the demand side, then sanity-check from the supply side."*
3. **Use round numbers** and say your assumptions out loud.
4. **Sanity-check** the answer and give a "so what".

*Example: cups of coffee sold by coffee shops in Shanghai per year*
- Demand side: population about 25m → ages 18–60 about 60% = 15m → about half drink shop coffee = 7.5m → about 3 cups a week ≈ 22m cups a week ≈ **1.1–1.2bn cups a year**
- Supply side: assume about 9,000 shops × about 300 cups a day × 365 ≈ **1.0bn**
- The two estimates agree, so the order of magnitude is about 1bn.

5. "What distinguishes interns who go on to receive **full-time offers**?"

**Avoid:** salary, working hours or anything easily found on the website. Also don't raise the 2024 layoffs or the U.S. political controversy.

## 3. McKinsey background

### 3.1 McKinsey globally (2026)

- Founded in **1926**, so 2026 is the **centennial year**. Global Managing Partner: **Bob Sternfels**.
- Workforce of roughly **40,000 people plus about 20,000–25,000 AI agents** (Sternfels' figures from Jan 2026, up from about 3,000 agents 18 months earlier).
- Hiring is up: Reuters reports McKinsey plans to grow its North American workforce by about 12% in 2026. Leadership has said younger hires bring stronger AI fluency, and that liberal-arts backgrounds were under-valued in the past.

### 3.2 McKinsey Greater China

- **Footprint.** Entered China in **1993** with offices in Beijing and Shanghai. Six locations today: **Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Hong Kong, Chengdu, Taipei**. There are 700+ partners, consultants and research analysts, plus 300+ professional staff, serving clients across 22+ industry sectors.
- **Leadership.**
  - **Joe Ngai:** Senior Partner and Chairman, Greater China
  - **Nick Leung:** Senior Partner, global board member and MGI lead director; chaired GCR for 12 years before Ngai
  - Other senior partners quoted in 2026: **Sheng Hong** (going global), **Lambert Bu** (AI), **Mingyu Guan** (family businesses)
- **Restructuring.** In **Oct 2024** McKinsey cut about 500 roles in China, roughly a third of the workforce there. It also separated the China business further from the global network, stopped work for local Chinese government bodies and scaled back state-owned enterprise (SOE) work. Headcount fell from about 1,500 (2023) to about 1,000.
- **Official client focus since then.** In McKinsey's words: *"today our work focuses on multinational and Chinese private sector firms."*
- **Generative AI restriction.** In **July 2025** the FT reported that McKinsey told its mainland China practice **not to take on generative-AI projects**, including for MNC clients' mainland offices. Work using more established forms of AI continues, and internal tools such as Lilli are global.
- **2026 tone.** Leung calls the addressable market "narrower" but fast-growing within that band, and Ngai calls the business "more mature".

### 3.3 Major client types in China

McKinsey does **not publish client names**. Never name or guess clients in an interview; talk about client *types* and *themes*.

| Client type | What McKinsey does for them (2025–26 themes) |
|---|---|
| **Multinationals in China** (consumer, luxury, auto, pharma, industrials, tech) | **"China for China" models** that give local teams autonomy and speed (Coach and Logitech are cited as examples); partnering with Chinese firms (VW, Stellantis); deciding whether to stay, grow, partner or sell (Starbucks and General Mills sold their China businesses to local investors). Leung advises running the China business as **a standalone business with its own capital, ownership and management**. |

| **Financial institutions** | Strategy, digital and risk work. Shanghai is the financial hub. |
| **SOEs and government** (historical) | Scaled back since 2024. This is the most politically sensitive part (U.S. congressional criticism). **Don't raise it**; if it comes up, say McKinsey has narrowed its focus to MNCs and private firms. |

### 3.4 McKinsey's view of China in 2026 (talking points)

- **"The Next China Is Still China"** (book, 16 Jun 2026; China Week, 9 Apr 2026, Shanghai)
  - There is no substitute for China, so boards need a China strategy. China is "the world's toughest gym": hard, competitive, oversupplied.
  - Growth is expected at about **4–5% a year** and trending lower. Leung calls this a **"mid-reset"**, not "the next Japan".
  - The next wave of growth **won't come from manufacturing**. Chinese consumers are "hyper-switching" with low brand loyalty.
- **Involution.** Competition is "at 11"; Ngai would like it at 7 or 8. About **30% of large industrial firms are loss-making**. Examples: BYD's repeated price cuts, and food-delivery subsidy wars between JD, Meituan and Alibaba (over ¥100bn across two quarters).
- **Going global.** Chinese firms had **50,000+ overseas branches in 190+ countries** by the end of 2025 (Ministry of Commerce). Outbound FDI is steady at about **$100bn a year**, broadening to Latin America, the Middle East and Europe. Examples: BYD sold over 1m cars overseas in 2025, plus Pop Mart/Labubu, *Black Myth: Wukong*, MiHoYo and Luckin in New York. The weakness is **strong products but weak brands**.
- **Trade.** China's share of global goods exports is holding at about **14%** despite U.S. tariffs of around 50%. Over half of exports go to the Global South. MGI says up to **30% of global trade could shift corridors by 2035**.
- **Consumers.** Discretionary spending is weak while tourism (+12%) and box office (+22%) grew in 2025. "Competing on price alone won't work." The *China Consumption Update: Year of the Horse* says winning now depends on **precise choices of segments, channels and offer design**, not broad presence.

## 4. AI at McKinsey and in China

### 4.1 How McKinsey itself uses AI (relevant to what you'd do day to day)

- **Lilli:** McKinsey's in-house gen-AI platform, launched Aug 2023 and built on 100 years of McKinsey IP. Used by **more than 70% of staff**. It searches knowledge, synthesises research and drafts documents, and is increasingly used for pages and charts that juniors used to build.
- **Agents:** about **20,000–25,000 AI agents** working alongside about 40,000 people. Sternfels expects every employee to be supported by one or more agents within about 18 months.
- **Staffing:** in May 2026, Bloomberg reported McKinsey plans to use AI agents to help match consultants to client teams, alongside the professional development managers.
- **Recruiting:** since Jan 2026, McKinsey has **piloted interviews where candidates use Lilli** on a case. Interviewers look at how you prompt the tool, **challenge its output** and fit it to the client's situation, testing "curiosity and judgment". It is reported as a non-evaluative pilot, mostly for business-school candidates; GCR use is unconfirmed (ask Daphne). In Apr 2026 McKinsey also launched a **free AI case-practice tool** for BA and Associate applicants.
- **QuantumBlack, AI by McKinsey:** the firm's AI and analytics arm (about 1,700 people). A senior partner has said AI-related work now makes up about 40% of the firm's work.
- **Implication for you:** the BA job is shifting from producing pages to **judgment, synthesis, checking AI output and working with clients**. That is a strong "why now" angle in your answers.

### 4.2 Latest McKinsey AI research

- ***The State of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI*** (25 Aug 2026; 1,719 respondents in 97 countries). Some figures below come from press coverage, so check the PDF before quoting precisely.
  - **40%** of large companies (revenue over $1bn) are **scaling AI agents**, up from 27% a year earlier. Smaller firms are flat at 22%.
  - Agents are scaled most in **IT, knowledge management and software engineering**.
  - **32%** have declined to buy at least one software product because they could build it in-house with agentic coding tools.
  - **80%** say AI improved their own productivity, but only **37%** see any EBIT impact (flat year on year). Only about **6%** are "AI high performers".
- ***The State of AI in 2025*** (Nov 2025): **88%** use AI regularly in at least one function (up from 78%), **23%** are scaling agents and another **39%** are experimenting with them, and **39%** report any EBIT impact.
- **The pattern McKinsey keeps repeating:** "pilot purgatory". Adoption is wide but value at scale is rare, and the bottleneck is **organisation and people, not technology**. Winners redesign workflows instead of layering pilots on old processes.

### 4.3 AI in China: McKinsey's view

- **Joe Ngai (Consensus, Feb 2026):** 98% implementing some AI, under 20% at scale, about 5% with measurable profit. Chinese firms are **more receptive to agentic AI**, with less legacy resistance and a decade of mobile and data digitisation behind them. The talk is about **usage, not models**. **Embodied AI** (robotics, autonomous driving) is the next frontier, and he predicts a **"robot dividend"** that offsets demographic decline.
- **China Week 2026:** in China, AI is used most for **knowledge management**, ahead of customer service and marketing (Lambert Bu). AI is "a general-purpose technology that will redefine work boundaries, not simply replace jobs" (Asutosh Padhi).
- **McKinsey China data:** about 85% of firms have deployed gen-AI in some form, but only **5–10% have moved past the pilot stage**. Earlier McKinsey work estimated **gen-AI could add about $2tn of economic value in China**.
- **Chinese AI going global:** open-source models from DeepSeek, Moonshot and MiniMax are winning global users. Ngai calls **AI tokens "the next Chinese export"** the U.S. hasn't found a way to tariff.
- **Sensitivity:** given the July 2025 restriction on gen-AI client projects in mainland China (§3.2), don't say "I want to do gen-AI projects for Chinese clients at McKinsey". Say instead that you're interested in **how companies capture value from AI and redesign how they work**, and in **how AI changes the analyst's job**.

### 4.4 A 30-second AI answer, if asked "what do you think about AI and consulting?"

> "McKinsey's own data shows adoption is no longer the issue: almost every company is experimenting, but only around 5–6% are capturing real profit impact, because the bottleneck is people and workflow redesign. That's exactly a consulting problem. For an analyst, tools like Lilli remove much of the manual research and slide work, so the value moves to asking the right question, challenging what the AI produces, and helping clients actually change. That's the skill set I want to build, and McKinsey is the place where that's already happening."

## 6. Scripts: Why consulting / Why McKinsey

Fill the `[brackets]` with **your own** examples. A real, specific example matters more than polished wording. Aim for 60–75 seconds each, and say the headline first.

### 6.1 Why consulting (English, about 60 seconds)

> I want to start my career in consulting for three reasons.
>
> **First, I enjoy structured problem-solving on messy, ambiguous questions.** When I `[experience, e.g. "worked on X during my internship at Y" / "led our team's Z project"]`, the part I enjoyed most was `[breaking a vague question into pieces, finding data to test each piece, and turning it into one clear recommendation]`. That's essentially what consultants do every day.
>
> **Second, the learning curve.** Consulting lets me see different industries and functions in a short time, get feedback constantly, and learn from people who are much better than me. I don't think any other early-career job compresses learning like that.
>
> **Third, impact.** I want my work to change what an organisation actually does. Right now Chinese companies are moving from growing *bigger* to growing *stronger*, and from exporting products to really operating overseas. Meanwhile multinationals are rethinking their China strategy. Helping leaders make those decisions is the work I want to do.
>
> So consulting is the best place for me to build that toolkit early in my career.

### 6.2 Why McKinsey (English, about 75 seconds)

> Three things draw me to McKinsey specifically.
>
> **First, the people and the apprenticeship model.** `[Optional: "When I spoke with ___ / attended the Life as a BA webinar…"]` What stood out was how much senior colleagues invest in juniors: frequent coaching, strengths-based development, and a mentor and a development manager looking after your growth. At the start of my career, that matters most to me.
>
> **Second, McKinsey's position in China.** McKinsey has been in China since 1993 and works with both multinationals and Chinese private companies. That puts the Shanghai office right at the intersection I care about: multinationals building "China for China" strategies, and Chinese companies going global. I've followed Joe Ngai and Nick Leung's argument that "the next China is still China", and `[your own one-line reaction or personal link, e.g. "having grown up in China and studied in the UK, I see both sides of that story"]`.
>
> **Third, how McKinsey is reinventing the work itself with AI**: Lilli, AI agents, QuantumBlack. The analyst's value is shifting from producing slides to judgment, synthesis and working with clients. I'd like to learn consulting where that change is already happening.
>
> That combination is why McKinsey Shanghai is where I most want to start.

### 6.4 Likely follow-ups (one-line answers)

| Follow-up | Answer direction |
|---|---|
| "Why not BCG or Bain?" | Never criticise them. Say what's distinctive about McKinsey: one-firm global partnership and scale, the depth of research and knowledge (MGI, Lilli), the apprenticeship culture, and 30+ years in China. Add what you heard from McKinsey people specifically. |
| "Why not banking or a corporate job?" | Consulting gives breadth across problems, not depth in one product or deal type. You want to learn *how to solve problems*, then decide where to specialise. |
| "What do you think a BA actually does?" | Owns a piece of the workstream: research and data, expert and client interviews, synthesis into pages, presenting to the client, all with daily coaching from the EM. |
| "What would you bring?" | Two strengths backed by evidence from your CV (e.g. analytical skill + bilingual and cross-cultural experience). |
| "Which industries interest you?" | Name one or two (e.g. consumer, auto/EV, Chinese brands going global) and say you're happy to be a generalist. |

**Avoid:** prestige, exit options, money, generic "I like learning", or listing McKinsey facts without linking them to yourself.
